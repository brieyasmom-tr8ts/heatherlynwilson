How it works

1

One verse, one letter

Every verse starts with its letter. A is for "Above all, love each other deeply." B is for "Be kind and compassionate." By Z, the alphabet is your memory cue.

2

A new verse every few days

A new verse every two to three days, with review days built in between. You are never starting from scratch.

3

Practice with the verse card

Each day your dashboard shows a verse card. Tap through full text, first letters only, and hidden to test yourself. Flag the ones that need more work.

4

Built for the whole family

Each verse includes a dinner-table question for kids. Short, specific, and actually worth asking. No curriculum knowledge required.

Why I made this

I wanted something simple enough to actually finish and structured enough to make the verses stick. A lot of memory programs front-load all the work and leave you scrambling. This one is different. New verses land every two to three days, review is built into the schedule, and the alphabet gives your brain a retrieval system it already knows.

The verses I chose are ones worth carrying. Not obscure. Not decorative. These are the ones I reach for when I am scared, grateful, stretched thin, or trying to figure out what love actually requires. By the time you reach Z, you will have a library of twenty-one verses you actually know, not twenty-one you sort of remember from somewhere.

Start it with your family. Start it with a friend. Start it by yourself. It is eight weeks, and it will be some of the best eight weeks you spend this year.

Heather
