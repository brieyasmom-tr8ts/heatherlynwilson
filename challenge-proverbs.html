How it works

1

One chapter a day

Proverbs has exactly 31 chapters. Read the day's chapter, or with little ones, just the few verses in that day's email. Either counts.

2

One big idea

Every day comes with one sentence that captures the chapter, so even the youngest kid walks away with something.

3

Questions for every age

Two questions for kids 5 to 10, two for ages 11 to 17. Real conversation starters, not homework.

4

One family challenge

A small doable thing each day: a kindness round at dinner, a code word for hard moments, a secret mission of generosity.

Why Proverbs, and why together

I am one of twelve kids, and I am raising a houseful of my own. I know exactly how family devotions usually go. Somebody is missing. Somebody is melting down. The plan that needed thirty quiet minutes never survives contact with real life.

So this one is built for real life. Ten minutes. One chapter, or even just a few verses read out loud by whichever kid grabs the phone first. One big idea. A couple of questions that actually start conversations, tuned for the littles and the teenagers. One small challenge to do together.

And it does not need a table. Some of the best conversations with my kids have happened in the car, where nobody has to make eye contact. Read it at breakfast, play it on the Bible app on the way to practice, text the question to your teenager. It all counts.

Proverbs was written by a parent to a child. Thirty-one chapters of a father saying listen, my son, here is how life actually works. It is the most family-shaped book in the Bible. Come read it with yours.
