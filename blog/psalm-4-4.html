I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Psalm 4:4

September 18, 2026
"Don't sin by letting anger control you. Think about it overnight and remain silent."Psalm 4:4 (NLT)

First off: anger controlling you is sin.

That is what this verse actually says. It does not say anger is sin. It does not say you cannot feel it. It says do not let it control you. There is a difference between feeling anger and letting anger have you.

And then it says something that I don't remember reading before.

Think about it overnight and remain silent.

Sleep on it. And be quiet in the meantime.

That is such practical wisdom. Especially the silent part. Because when I am angry, the last thing I want to be is silent. I want to talk and argue. I pick up my phone immediately to type the reply. I want to tell someone "my side" and then defend myself. I want to explain why I am right and they are wrong.

But the verse says wait. Sleep first. Do not react in the middle of it.

Now I know there is another verse people quote all the time from Ephesians. Do not let the sun go down on your anger. That one is important too. It is warning us not to let anger take root and turn into bitterness. Not to carry it for days and weeks and years. Not to nurse it and feed it and let it become part of who we are.

Both are true.

I actually think they work together.

Sometimes a good night of sleep does something to anger that arguing in the moment never could. It gives it a chance to settle. It lets you wake up with fresh eyes. It gives your brain a chance to actually process what happened instead of just reacting to what happened. Most of the time, the thing that felt so intense at ten p.m. does not feel nearly as sharp at seven a.m.

Silence and sleep have a way of separating the truth from the emotion.

And on the other side, the Ephesians principle keeps the sleeping honest. You are not sleeping on it so you can bury it and pretend it did not happen. You are sleeping on it so you can wake up and handle it well. There is still a job to do. You still have to address the thing. You just address it with a calmer head.

That is the balance.

Feel your anger. Do not deny it. Do not stuff it. But do not let it run the show either. And do not let it stay past its welcome. Sleep on it. Stay silent. Come back to it. Deal with it. Then let it go.

If we really lived by this verse, so many of our relationships would look different.

Most of the damage we do when we are angry could have been avoided if we had just been quiet long enough for the fire to cool.

The silent part is the hard part.

That is really what I struggle with. It is one thing to say I am going to sleep on it. It is another thing to actually not say a word until you have. Not send the text, fire off the email or vent to a friend just to keep it going. Not stew out loud and replay over and over. Silent means silent.

I want to be that kind of person.

The kind who feels the anger honestly. Recognizes the anger then sits with it. Sleeps on it if necessary and then handles it with a clear heart instead of a hot one.

That is not weakness it is wisdom.

What is something you have been angry about that could probably use a night of sleep and some silence before you respond?
