I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Mercy Received

August 26, 2026
"The king took off his signet ring—which he had taken back from Haman—and gave it to Mordecai. And Esther appointed Mordecai to be in charge of Haman's property."Esther 8:2 (NLT)
"In every province and city, wherever the king's decree arrived, the Jews rejoiced and had a great celebration and declared a public festival and holiday. And many of the people of the land became Jews themselves, for they feared what the Jews might do to them."Esther 8:17 (NLT)

What a moment.

If you back up and look at the whole picture, the situation had looked impossible. Haman had convinced the king to wipe out the Jewish people. The decree had already been written. Sealed with the king's ring. Sent out across the empire. There was no way to undo a Persian decree once it had been issued.

It looked like the end.

And then God moved.

Esther stepped in. Mordecai was honored. Haman was hung on the very gallows he had built. And now in chapter 8, the king takes the signet ring off his own hand and hands it to Mordecai. The very ring Haman had been using to seal death warrants is now in the hand of the man Haman had been trying to kill.

That is the kind of reversal only God does.

And then the Jewish people get the news. The decree is reversed. They are saved. They are going to live. The whole story turns from impending genocide to celebration in a matter of chapters.

Verse 17 is one of the highlights for me at first.

The Jews rejoiced. They had a great celebration. They declared a public festival and holiday. I love picturing that. Streets full of people who had been bracing for the worst, now celebrating because their God had moved on their behalf.

But then the verse keeps going.

And many of the people of the land became Jews themselves, for they feared what the Jews might do to them.

That last part is where it got interesting.

Because what was happening was this. The Jews had just been rescued. They had just been spared. They had just received mercy from God in a way that was almost unbelievable. And then on the other side of that rescue, the way they handled their newfound power scared everyone else so much that people were converting just to stay safe.

Read that again.

The people God had just delivered, the people who had just received mercy, were so brutal with everyone around them that fear spread through the empire.

I wanted to just brush over that, but instead I highlighted it. lol.

You would think that experiencing that kind of rescue would have changed how they treated the people around them. You would think that mercy received would lead to mercy given. You would think that being spared something brutal would make them careful not to be brutal themselves.

But that does not seem to be what happened.

I am not saying the Jews were wrong to defend themselves. The decree allowed them to fight back against those who had been planning to attack them. That part is in the story. But the way verse 17 phrases it makes you stop. People did not convert because they were drawn to God. They converted because they were afraid of what God's people might do to them.

That is a very different kind of fear than the fear of the Lord.

And it makes me think about how often we forget where we came from.

How often we receive mercy and turn around and withhold it from someone else. How often we get rescued and become hard. How often we forget what we were spared from and start treating other people in the very way we ourselves were just delivered out of.

The Jews had just been on the wrong end of an unjust decree. And the verse hints that they may have swung the other way pretty quickly.

I do not want to be like that.

I want to receive mercy and turn into a more merciful person. I want to be delivered and become someone who looks for ways to deliver others. I want my rescue to soften me, not harden me.

That is the thread I keep coming back to in this story. The reversal was beautiful. God moved in a way that only He could. But once the power shifted, the question became what kind of people are we going to be on this side of it?

That is a question we still have to answer today.

When God rescues us out of something, do we become more merciful, or do we just turn around and use our new strength the same way our enemies were planning to use theirs?
