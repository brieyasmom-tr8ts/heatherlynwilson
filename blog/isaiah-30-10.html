I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Tell Us Nice Things

November 13, 2026
"They tell the seers, 'Stop seeing visions!' They tell the prophets, 'Don't tell us what is right. Tell us nice things. Tell us lies.'"Isaiah 30:10 (NLT)

This is exactly what society is saying right now.

Do not tell us what is right. Tell us what feels good. Do not tell us the truth. Tell us what we want to hear. Do not challenge us. Do not correct us. Do not make us uncomfortable. Just say nice things. And if the nice things aren't true, that is fine. We will take the lies.

Thousands of years ago, this was the posture of the people God was warning. And thousands of years later, it is the posture of most of our culture.

You see it everywhere. Preachers being pushed out because they won't soften what the Bible actually says. Truth being called hate. Correction being called judgment. Boundaries being called cruelty. Sin being called love. And anyone who dares to name what is actually true gets labeled the villain.

We have decided as a society that our feelings are the highest authority. If a truth doesn't match our feelings, we would rather change the truth than change our feelings. That is a dangerous place to be.

Because feelings aren't stable. They shift. They lie. They change with the wind. And a whole culture built on making feelings the standard is a culture that will crumble under any real pressure.

The people in Isaiah's day were doing the same thing. And what happened to them is instructive. God didn't give them the lies they were asking for. He kept sending true prophets. He kept sending His Word. He kept warning them, even when they didn't want to hear it.

Because a real God who loves His people doesn't tell them lies just because they prefer them.

A real God tells the truth.

Even when it stings. Even when it costs. Even when the crowd hates it. Even when the culture pressures Him to soften it.

That is what love actually looks like. Not agreement with whatever we want to be true. Not endless validation. Real love tells the truth. Real love risks the relationship to say what needs to be said. Real love doesn't just hand you the nice version. It gives you the actual version.

I want to be someone who wants the truth. Not just when it is easy. Not just when it lines up with what I already think. Even when it corrects me. Even when it stings. Even when I'd rather someone just tell me nice things and let me keep going the way I was going.

Because a life built on truth stands. A life built on comfortable lies falls apart.

Where in your life have you been asking God to tell you nice things when what you actually need is the truth?
