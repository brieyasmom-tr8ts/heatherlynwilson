I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

While He Was Still Speaking

August 31, 2026
"While he was still speaking, another messenger arrived with this news: 'The fire of God has fallen from heaven and burned up your sheep and all the shepherds. I am the only one who escaped to tell you.' While he was still speaking, another messenger arrived with this news: 'Three bands of Chaldean raiders have stolen your camels and killed your servants. I am the only one who escaped to tell you.' While he was still speaking, another messenger arrived with this news: 'Your sons and daughters were feasting in their oldest brother's home...'"Job 1:16-18 (NLT)

What hit me when I read these verses was the phrasing.

While he was still speaking.

It says it three times in a row. One messenger has not even finished telling Job the bad news before another shows up with worse news. And then another. And then another.

Boom. Sheep gone. Shepherds gone.

Boom. Camels gone. Servants gone.

Boom. All of his sons and daughters. Gone.

There was no pause. No moment to process. No time to brace. Just wave after wave of devastation, faster than he could even take it in.

I have always thought about that.

Because life can feel like that sometimes. You are still in the middle of one hard thing when the next one shows up. You are still trying to process one loss and another walks through the door. You are still on the phone about one crisis and the next call is coming in.

While you are still speaking, another messenger arrives.

It feels almost cruel when it stacks like that. Like you cannot even get your feet under you before the next blow knocks you down again. In a season like that in my life, although not near as devastating as Job's situation, I remember asking God, why are you picking on me?

That is what was happening to Job.

And we know what comes next in the story. The long chapters with his friends. The wrestling. The questions. The grief that lasts and lasts. But before any of that, Job does something I never really noticed.

The very next verse says he stood up, tore his robe, shaved his head, and fell to the ground in worship.

Worship.

That is the first thing he does.

Not anger. Not blame. Not denial. He does not pretend it is fine. He tears his robe and shaves his head, which were both ancient ways of expressing deep grief. He is not holding back. He is mourning hard. But in the middle of that grief, the posture he takes is to fall to the ground in worship.

He grieves and worships at the same time.

That is one of the most powerful things in the whole book.

We so often think of worship and grief as opposites. Like you have to feel okay before you can worship. Like you have to get past the pain to lift your hands. But Job did not separate them. He let them happen in the same moment.

He wept. He worshipped. Both at once.

And then he said one of the most quoted lines in all of Scripture. The Lord gave me what I had, and the Lord has taken it away. Praise the name of the Lord.

He did not pretend the pain was not real. He did not say it is fine, God knows best. He acknowledged the loss. The Lord has taken it away. And then he praised Him anyway.

I want that kind of heart.

The kind that does not have to wait until the storm passes to worship. The kind that can fall on its face in grief and in praise at the same time. The kind that does not blame God or run from Him when the messengers keep coming, but turns toward Him in the very moment of the loss.

Because that is the moment that matters most.

What we do in the loss reveals more about us than what we do once the loss is behind us. And Job, in his very first reaction, before he had time to think it through, before he had time to talk himself into anything, just dropped to the ground in worship.

The worship was already there, underneath, long before the messengers showed up. The disaster did not give him a new heart. It revealed the one he already had.

What is in you when the messengers keep coming?
