I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Careful

July 20, 2026
"King Jotham became powerful because he was careful to live in obedience to the Lord his God."2 Chronicles 27:6 (NLT)

This is the son of the king I wrote about in the last blog.

Uzziah. The one who started off so well and ended in pride and disgrace. The one who walked into the temple thinking he was above the rules and walked out with leprosy.

Now we meet his son Jotham.

And the Bible says something so important about him. It says he did what was right in the sight of the Lord, just as his father had done. However, he did not enter the temple of the Lord.

He did what his father did. Except for the sin.

He took the good. And he left the bad.

That is one of the most hopeful sentences in this whole section of Scripture.

Because we do not have to repeat the failures of the generations before us.

That is not always easy to believe. There is a lot of teaching out there about generational patterns and family cycles and how the same struggles repeat themselves over and over in a bloodline. And some of that is true. We do pick things up from the people who raised us. We do inherit habits and tendencies and even wounds.

But the story of Jotham is the story of a son who decided that what tripped his father up was not going to trip him up.

He honored the parts of his father's life worth honoring. The pursuit of God. The leadership. The accomplishments. The good legacy was kept.

But when it came to the place where his father fell, Jotham drew a line.

There is one word in this verse that I do not want to skip past.

Careful.

King Jotham became powerful because he was careful to live in obedience to the Lord his God.

Not accidentally obedient. Not naturally obedient. Careful.

That word means it was intentional. Watchful. Guarded. He paid attention to his life. He paid attention to his steps. He paid attention to the place his father went wrong, and he made sure he did not drift there himself.

That is not how most of us live.

We mostly just go. We mostly just react. We mostly just do the next thing in front of us and hope it adds up to a good life. We are not careful. We are not watchful. We are not paying close attention to the patterns in our family or the line we promised God we were not going to cross.

Jotham was different. He watched. He guarded. He chose.

And the Bible directly ties his power to that carefulness. He became powerful because he was careful to obey.

His blessing came from his intentionality.

We do not break cycles by hoping things turn out differently. We break them by being careful. By noticing where the pull is strongest. By naming the patterns. By saying out loud, that was my dad's struggle. It will not be mine. Not because I am stronger. Because I am paying attention.

If your parents struggled with anger, be careful. If they drank, be careful. If they avoided God, be careful. If they walked away from the church, be careful. If they were unfaithful, be careful. If they ran, be careful.

You are not stuck. But you do have to be aware.

The patterns can break. The cycles can stop. But it takes carefulness. The kind Jotham had. The kind that does not drift but actually chooses, day after day, to live the opposite of the wrong example.

What is one place in your life where you need to be more careful than you have been?
