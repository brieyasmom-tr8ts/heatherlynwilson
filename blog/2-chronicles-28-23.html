Highlighted

I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

2 Chronicles 28:23

July 22, 2026
"He offered sacrifices to the gods of Damascus who had defeated him, for he said, 'Since these gods helped the kings of Aram, they will help me, too, if I sacrifice to them.' But instead, they led to his ruin and the ruin of all Israel."2 Chronicles 28:23 (NLT)

This story is so backwards it almost does not make sense.

King Ahaz of Judah is in trouble. The Arameans have just defeated him. His army got crushed. His people are suffering. He is losing on every side.

So what does he do?

He decides to start worshipping the gods of the people who just beat him.

Read that again.

He looked at the army that just destroyed him and thought well, their gods must be working. If I worship those gods too, maybe they will help me the way they helped them.

So he sacrificed to the gods of his enemies. The ones who had nothing to do with the God of Israel. The ones who actually had nothing to do with the victory at all. And he tried to use them to fix his life.

The verse says they led to his ruin and the ruin of all Israel.

I think about how often we do this exact thing.

We look around at people who seem successful. People whose lives look like they are working. People who have the money, the marriage, the platform, the followers, the house, the body, the calm and put-together image. And we copy them.

Even if they do not love God. Even if their whole foundation is built on something other than Him. We figure if it works for them, it will work for us.

So we adopt their habits. Their priorities. Their ways of thinking. Their version of success. We try to bring their gods into our house, thinking it might give us what they have.

And it ruins us.

Just like it ruined Ahaz.

Because what we cannot see is that someone else's so-called success is not the whole story. We are seeing the highlight reel. We are not seeing the emptiness. We are not seeing the broken relationships. We are not seeing the anxiety, the addiction, the spiritual deadness, the slow drift away from anything real.

We see the win. And we want it.

So we sacrifice to their gods.

We try the thing that worked for them, hoping it will work for us, even when we know deep down it is not from God.

But borrowed gods do not bless. They consume.

They led to ruin then. And they lead to ruin now.

The thing that struck me about Ahaz is that he had access to the real God. He was a king of Judah. His own father had walked with God. He had the temple. He had the priests. He had the scrolls. He had every resource available to seek the Lord.

But he chose foreign gods instead.

I do not want to be that.

I do not want to chase the formulas of people who look successful on the outside while ignoring the God I already have access to. I do not want to copy somebody else's life because it looks like it is working. I do not want to drift into worship of things God never told me to worship just because the world keeps telling me they are the answer.

The God who has been with me from the beginning is the God who knows the way through.

I do not need to borrow somebody else's.

Where in your life have you been tempted to copy someone whose foundation is not God, hoping it will work for you too?
