Job's Regular Practice

August 28, 2026
"When these celebrations ended—sometimes after several days—Job would purify his children. He would get up early in the morning and offer a burnt offering for each of them. For Job said to himself, 'Perhaps my children have sinned and have cursed God in their hearts.' This was Job's regular practice."Job 1:5 (NLT)

I remember reading this verse a few years ago.

My husband and I had just decided to start praying and journaling for our kids in a more dedicated way. Not just the kind of prayers you whisper when you are worried. Not just the God bless them today type. Actual regular intentional prayer for each of them.

And during that time I read this verse about Job. I do not remember noticing it before.

Long before he lost everything. Long before the famous suffering. Long before the friends with the bad advice. He was just a dad who got up early in the morning to intercede for his kids.

He would get up early. Offer sacrifices on behalf of each one of his children. Just in case they had sinned. Just in case their hearts had drifted. He did not know what they had done. He did not need to know. He just covered them in prayer before God on a regular basis.

The verse says this was Job's regular practice.

It was not a one-time thing. It was not a panic prayer when something went wrong. It was a rhythm. A pattern. Something he just did.

As a mom, I know firsthand that you cannot always know what is going on in your kids' hearts. You can ask. You can listen. You can pay attention. But you cannot really see in there. You do not know what is being thought, what is being struggled with, what is being prayed about, what is being avoided.

Only God knows.

So Job did the only thing a parent really can do. He went to the One who actually sees.

He brought them before God on a regular basis. Not because he had proof of their failure. Not because he was assuming the worst. Just because he knew the human heart and he wanted them covered.

That is what prayer for our kids really is. It is not us trying to control them. It is not us trying to fix them. It is us bringing them, again and again, before the only One who can actually do anything about what is going on inside of them.

It does not have to be elaborate. It does not have to be long. It just has to be regular.

Get up early. Pray over them. Cover them. Trust that God hears it and is at work in ways you cannot see.

There is something so steadying about that for me. It helps me recognize I am not in control but I know the God who is. It is actually freeing. Because there are seasons when I cannot reach my kids the way I want to. There are seasons when they are pulling away or making choices I would not make for them or going through something I cannot fix. And in all of those seasons, prayer is the one thing I can always do.

And God hears.

He has been hearing parents pray for their kids for thousands of years. Job's prayers were heard. Hannah's prayers were heard. So many faithful parents have been on their knees before us, and God has been faithful to them.

He will be faithful to us too.

If you are a parent, do you have a regular practice of praying for your kids, or have you been leaving it to those times when something is wrong?
