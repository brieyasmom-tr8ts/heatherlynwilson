Highlighted

I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Proverbs 30:4

October 30, 2026
"Who but God goes up to heaven and comes back down? Who holds the wind in his fists? Who wraps up the oceans in his cloak? Who has created the whole wide world? What is his name — and his son's name? Tell me if you know!"Proverbs 30:4 (NLT)

Read that last line again.

What is his name and his son's name?

That is in Proverbs. In the Old Testament. Hundreds of years before Jesus was born. And the writer is casually asking, do you know God's name? And do you know His Son's name?

That stood out to me, so I highlighted it.

Because we tend to think of Jesus as a New Testament reveal. Like He shows up in Matthew and the story of God having a Son begins. But this verse says otherwise. The Son was always there. Always part of the story. The Old Testament writers had hints and glimpses. They were pointing to Him long before He arrived.

This isn't the only place either. Isaiah 9 talks about a child being born who would be called Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace. Daniel had visions of the Son of Man. The Psalms speak of the Anointed One. Threads of Jesus are woven through the entire Old Testament, if you know to look for them.

Proverbs 30:4 is one of those threads.

It is a question the writer knew he couldn't fully answer at the time. But he asked it anyway. Almost like he was leaning toward something he could sense but couldn't yet see clearly.

We get to know the answer.

His name is God. And His Son's name is Jesus.

The whole Bible was pointing to Him all along. And when I catch a glimpse like this in a chapter I wouldn't have expected, it reminds me how intentional God has been from the very beginning. He was never scrambling. He was never making it up as He went. The plan of Jesus was there in Genesis, in Proverbs, in Isaiah, in Daniel. He was always the answer to every question the Old Testament was slowly asking.

Where else in the Old Testament have you seen a glimpse of Jesus that you did not notice before?
