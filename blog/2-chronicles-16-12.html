He Did Not Even Pray

July 8, 2026
"In the thirty-ninth year of his reign, Asa developed a serious foot disease. Yet even with the severity of his disease, he did not seek the Lord's help but turned only to his physicians."2 Chronicles 16:12 (NLT)

I think I highlighted this one as a reminder.

We are blessed to have doctors. We are blessed to have medicine and surgery and specialists who can diagnose things our ancestors would have just died from. I am thankful for physicians. Truly.

But this verse is not saying doctors are wrong.

It is saying Asa turned only to his physicians.

Only.

He did not turn to the Lord at all.

And what stood out to me is that a lot of people, when something serious happens, that is when they finally seek God. A scary diagnosis. A bad accident. A test that comes back wrong. Hard things have a way of softening people and turning their eyes upward.

But Asa did not even do that.

He had a serious disease. The kind of thing that would normally drive a person to their knees. And he just went to the doctors. He did not pray. He did not seek God. He did not even cry out.

That is part of what makes this story so sad.

Because this is the same Asa who, earlier in his reign, cried out to God when an enemy army of a million men came against him. He prayed Lord, no one but you can help the powerless against the mighty. And God showed up.

Same king. Different posture.

When he was younger, he ran to God first. When he was older, he ran to God last. Or in this case, not at all.

That progression is sobering.

It is so easy to slowly stop including God in the things that matter. To trust the doctor instead of asking the doctor and the Lord. To trust the expert instead of seeking the One who made the expert. To go straight to the solution and skip past the One who can actually heal.

I do not want to be that person.

I want to be someone who prays and goes to the doctor.

Someone who asks God for wisdom, and reads the book.

Someone who seeks Him first and then gets the advice.

Not instead of help. Alongside it. Above it.

Because the help is good. But the Lord is the one who decides how the help actually plays out.

When something hard hits, where do you go first?
