I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Never Again

November 25, 2026
"Just as I swore in the time of Noah that I would never again let a flood cover the earth, so now I swear that I will never again be angry and punish you."Isaiah 54:9 (NLT)

At first glance this verse can be confusing. Because we know God does get angry. We know He does bring judgment. The Bible is full of it. So how can He say He will never again be angry and punish His people?

The answer, I think, points straight to Jesus.

God does get angry at sin. That hasn't changed. He is holy, and He can't look at sin and shrug it off. There is a real cost to sin, a real punishment that has to be paid. His justice demands it. That is who He is.

But He also provided the sacrifice that covered the payment owed.

That is the whole story of the cross. God didn't just decide to stop being angry. He isn't soft on sin. What He did was pour out the full weight of that anger on Jesus so that we would never have to carry it. Jesus took the flood in our place.

That is what makes this promise possible. God isn't saying sin doesn't matter anymore. He is saying that for His people, the payment has already been made. The wrath has been poured out and the judgment handled. There is no more punishment left for those who are covered by the blood of Jesus.

Just like He swore never to flood the earth again after Noah, He swears here that His people who belong to Him won't face that kind of wrath anymore. Because Jesus already did.

That doesn't mean life is easy, that we never face consequences for our choices, or that we don't still walk through hard things. But it means the eternal punishment we deserved has already been dealt with. The relationship is restored, and the wrath is gone.

That is one of the most beautiful truths of the whole Bible.

His anger and His justice are real, and His love is real enough that He satisfied both by giving His own Son.

We don't have to live in fear of God's wrath if we are His. That has been handled. What we get to live in now is His faithful love that endures forever.

Have you ever really let the weight of that sink in, that Jesus took the wrath so you could live in peace with the Father?
