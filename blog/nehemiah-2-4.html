Pray Before You Speak

August 10, 2026
"The king asked, 'Well, how can I help you?' With a prayer to the God of heaven,"Nehemiah 2:4 (NLT)

Nehemiah is one of the most famous books in the Bible. And one of the most famous people in it. He rebuilt the wall of Jerusalem. He led an exiled people back into purpose. He stood up to enemies who wanted to stop the work. He is the kind of person leadership books still write about thousands of years later.

And his whole story starts right here.

With a prayer.

Before he ever spoke to the king. Before he ever made the request that would change history. Before he ever picked up a tool or organized a team or stood in front of a single person. He paused, in the middle of a tense conversation with the most powerful man in the kingdom, and prayed.

It is such a small detail.

Half a verse.

With a prayer to the God of heaven.

You could almost read right past it. But I think that is what made me highlight this verse.

Because before that quick prayer, Nehemiah was just a cupbearer with a heavy heart. After that quick prayer, he became the man God used to rebuild a city.

That short pause changed everything.

I keep thinking about how often we make our requests without praying first.

We open the email and start typing the ask.

We pick up the phone and dial.

We sit down at the meeting and pitch.

We have the conversation with the spouse and bring up the hard thing.

We talk to the boss about the raise.

We confront the friend.

We ask the favor.

And in between every step, where there could have been a quiet prayer, there is just us. Just our nerves. Just our plan. Just our words. We are doing the talking and God is somewhere in the background, kind of like a wallpaper we have stopped noticing.

Nehemiah did not do that.

Right in front of the king, in the middle of the most important moment of his life so far, he stopped. Internally. Silently. Quickly. And he prayed before he answered.

Nobody else in the room knew.

But God knew.

And the answer Nehemiah gave the king after that prayer was the answer that set everything in motion.

I want to be the kind of person who prays before I speak.

Not the long prayers. Not the rehearsed prayers. Just the God, help me kind of prayer. The Lord, give me the words kind. The show me what to do right now kind. The kind you can say in the half second between someone asking you a question and you opening your mouth to answer.

That kind of prayer is available to us all day long. And it might be the kind that changes more than anything else.

Because the people God uses are not always the most gifted or the most prepared. They are the ones who keep including Him in the small moments. The ones who pause. The ones who lift their heart up even when no one would know.

The whole Nehemiah story is built on a man who prayed first.

What request are you about to make that you have not actually prayed about yet?
