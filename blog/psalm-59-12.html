Highlighted

I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Psalm 59:12

October 7, 2026
"Because of the sinful things they say, because of the evil that is on their lips, let them be captured by their pride, their curses, and their lies."Psalm 59:12 (NLT)

We live in a world that celebrates pride.

Pride month. Pride parades. Pride in every color and category you can imagine. Pride isn't just tolerated in our culture. It is applauded. It is marketed. It is packaged and sold back to us as strength, as identity, as freedom.

But this verse says the opposite.

David isn't celebrating pride. He is watching what it does to people. And he uses this word that stopped me.

Captured.

Pride doesn't free people. It captures them.

That is a very different picture than the one our culture is painting. The world says pride is what sets you free. God's Word says pride is what locks you up. And when I look around, I can see which one is actually true.

Pride captures people in their own opinion of themselves. It keeps them from being teachable or correctable. It keeps them from admitting they were wrong, asking for help, or apologizing. It keeps them from repenting, from bowing to anyone or anything, even the God who made them.

That isn't freedom.

That is a cage.

And David goes even further. He says the same thing about their curses and their lies. The words that come out of a proud mouth eventually turn back on the person who spoke them. What they said about others starts to come true about themselves. What they meant for harm ends up doing damage to the very person who said it.

There is a kind of poetic justice to it. You don't have to fight the proud or answer every curse spoken over you. God has a way of letting people be caught up in the very traps they were trying to set.

Their pride captures them.

Their curses come back on them.

Their lies expose them.

I've watched this play out in real life more times than I can count. In news stories. In friendships. In family. In leadership. Someone rides high on their own pride for a while, and eventually the very thing they built their name on becomes the thing that unravels them. Not always instantly. Sometimes it takes years. But it happens.

Because pride isn't sustainable. It is a heavy thing to carry, and eventually it crushes the one carrying it.

Which is exactly why God keeps warning us about it.

He isn't being harsh when He tells us to walk in humility. He is being kind. He is saving us from being captured by something we thought was strength. He is inviting us into a way of living that actually leads to freedom, not to a cage dressed up in celebration.

Humility is the exit. It is the way out of the trap pride sets. It is the posture that lets God lift us up instead of forcing our own way to the top and eventually collapsing under our own weight.

I don't want to be captured.

Not by my pride. Not by my words. Not by anything I'm trying to convince myself is freedom while it is actually building a cage around me.

I want the freedom that comes from bowing low.

Where in your life might pride be holding you captive in a way you are calling freedom?
