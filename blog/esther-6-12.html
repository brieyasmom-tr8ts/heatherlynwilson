Why I Highlighted This One

August 24, 2026
"Afterward Mordecai returned to the palace gate, but Haman hurried home dejected and completely humiliated."Esther 6:12 (NLT)

I am sitting here trying to figure out why I highlighted this one.

If I am being honest with you, I think it is because there is a part of me that likes this verse.

Haman was awful. He was scheming to wipe out an entire people. He had a gallows built specifically to kill Mordecai. He was full of pride and rage and entitlement. And in this exact moment, God flipped the whole thing on him.

Haman ends up having to lead Mordecai through the streets, dressed in royal robes, announcing his honor.

And then this verse.

Haman hurried home dejected and completely humiliated.

There is a part of me that reads that and thinks good.

That is the honest answer. There is something in me that likes to see the bad guy humbled. Brought low. Embarrassed. I want to dress it up in spiritual language, but underneath it, it is just my flesh enjoying somebody getting what they had coming.

BTW, I do not think that is a great impulse.

I know what the Bible says. Do not rejoice when your enemies fall. Love your enemies. Do good to those who hate you. I know all of that. But sitting here being honest about why I highlighted this verse, I think it was because that part of me that wants to see the villain knocked down a peg got fed in this story.

And I am not sure what to do with that.

It does not feel super blog-worthy. It is not a tidy spiritual takeaway. It is just me admitting that my flesh response and my spirit response do not always line up. That I cheer for things sometimes that I probably should not be cheering for. That justice and revenge can feel really similar from the inside, and I do not always sort out which one is in my heart.

Maybe this is the bigger thing.

There is a difference between God bringing justice and me wanting someone to suffer. They can look similar from the outside, but they come from very different places. God was righteous in humbling Haman. The story is not wrong. God's justice is part of who He is.

But my response to it. That is where I have to be careful.

Because if my heart is leaping at Haman's humiliation more than it is bowing at God's faithfulness, something is off. The story is not really about Haman. It is about God protecting His people. It is about a quiet faithfulness behind the scenes that turned everything around.

And I should be in awe of that. Not just smug about Haman.

So maybe the takeaway for me is just noticing. Noticing where my flesh likes to land. Noticing the difference between celebrating God's justice and enjoying someone else's pain. Noticing that even good Bible stories can stir up things in my heart that need to be examined.

I do not think I have this one figured out.

But I am glad I can be honest about it instead of pretending I read this verse and only had holy thoughts. And then I get to bring it before God and ask Him to continue working on me.

Have you ever caught yourself reacting to a story or situation in a way that did not really line up with the heart of God?
