I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Psalm 18:39

September 21, 2026
"You have armed me with strength for the battle; you have subdued my enemies under my feet."Psalm 18:39 (NLT)

I really like this verse.

Specifically the phrase you have armed me with strength for the battle.

Because it names something I've felt.

I'm not just naturally strong going into a battle. I don't walk around ready for a fight. Honestly, I hate fighting. I'd avoid most confrontation if I could. My default is peacemaker, or at least laugh maker, not warrior.

But there have been seasons where I've had to be strong. Not because I found it in myself. Because God armed me with it.

That is what this verse says.

He arms me.

He doesn't just tell me to go be strong on my own. He doesn't throw me into a battle empty handed. He equips me. He hands me the strength I don't naturally have. He fits it to me like armor before I walk out into the fight.

That completely changes how I think about hard seasons.

Because there have been times I've looked back and thought how did I even get through that? The years I couldn't have handled if you had described them to me ahead of time. The conversations I'd have run from. The situations that required more than I had in me. And somehow, I stood. Somehow, I made it through. Somehow, I even walked through them with some grace.

That wasn't me.

That was God arming me.

He gave me strength I didn't manufacture, words I didn't prepare, a steadiness that wasn't naturally in my personality, and a courage way beyond my normal capacity.

He armed me.

For the battle. In the moment I actually needed it. Not before. Not after. Right when I stepped into it.

That is such a comforting truth for anyone who feels like they aren't the type of person who can handle what is in front of them.

Because David, the guy writing this psalm, was a warrior. He had killed a giant. He had led armies. He had been through more battles than most of us can imagine. And even he didn't credit himself. He said You armed me.

If David had to be armed by God, then so do I. So do you.

We aren't supposed to walk into our battles trusting in our own strength. We are supposed to walk in trusting the One who arms us. And when we do, we find that we are stronger in the fight than we were sitting on the sidelines.

The strength shows up when the battle does.

I've had to learn that. Sometimes I sit and worry about a hard conversation or a hard season coming up and I think I don't have what it takes for that. And I'm usually right. I don't. Not on my own. But God doesn't give me the strength while I'm sitting in my kitchen worrying about it. He gives it when I actually step into it.

That is His pattern.

He arms me for the battle. In the battle. Not before.

I hate fighting. I'd rather peace. But I'm glad that when the battles come, I'm not standing there empty handed. My strength isn't from me. It is placed on me by a God who has walked me through every fight I've ever been in.

And He will do it again.

What battle are you dreading right now, and can you trust that God will arm you for it when the moment actually comes?
