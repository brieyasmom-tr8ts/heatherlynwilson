I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Proverbs 18:13

October 26, 2026
"Spouting off before listening to the facts is both shameful and foolish."Proverbs 18:13 (NLT)

This is another one of those verses that reads like it was written for the exact culture we are living in.

We spout off constantly. Post before we research. Repost the article we never actually read. Share the meme without checking if a word of it is true. Form a strong opinion in about four seconds and then broadcast it to everyone we know.

And Proverbs calls it what it is. Foolish.

Not just unwise. Not just a bad habit. Shameful and foolish.

That word shameful is important because we don't really think of it that way anymore. We have made a whole culture out of quick takes and strong reactions. We reward the loudest voices, not the most thoughtful ones. Being the first to comment feels more important than being the most informed.

But God isn't impressed with how fast we can talk.

It shows up in our personal conversations too. How often are we actually listening when someone is talking to us? Or are we just waiting for our turn to share what we already think? Are we asking questions to learn, or asking them to steer the conversation to what we want to say next?

Real listening is rare. And it is one of the most Christ-like things a person can do.

Jesus asked questions constantly. He let people talk. He listened. He drew things out of them. He didn't rush to correct or fix or explain. He was fully present in conversations in a way most of us aren't, because our minds are already three steps ahead planning our response.

The hard part is that this verse is convicting for me. Because I do this too. I've opinions. I want to share them. I can spout off with the best of them if I'm not careful. And every time I do, I usually regret it. Not because I was wrong, necessarily, but because I skipped the listening part.

Listening first is humility. Talking first is pride.

That is really what this verse is about. It is one more reminder that in a world that rewards volume, God still rewards wisdom. And wisdom takes its time. It hears people out. Asks real questions. Waits before it says a word.

I want to be more of that kind of person. Quicker to listen. Slower to react before I actually understand what I'm reacting to.

Where in your life have you been spouting off when God is inviting you to listen first?
