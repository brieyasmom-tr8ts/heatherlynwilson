Do We Still Blush?

August 7, 2026
"I prayed, 'O my God, I am utterly ashamed; I blush to lift up my face to you. For our sins are piled higher than our heads, and our guilt has reached to the heavens.'"Ezra 9:6 (NLT)

Ezra is praying, and his prayer is one of the most raw confessions in the Bible.

I am utterly ashamed. I blush to lift up my face to You. Our sins are piled higher than our heads.

That is the posture that stood out to me.

Do we blush in shame over our sin anymore?

Do we even recognize it as shameful? Or have we gotten so used to the things we do, the things we excuse, the things we participate in, that we walk through life without ever once being undone by what we are actually doing?

I think this is one of the biggest losses in our generation.

We have gotten so good at managing the appearance of being okay that we forget to be honest about what is actually piling up inside of us.

We live in fake confidence. I am good. I am fine. I am better than most. I am doing the best I can. It is not that big of a deal. Everyone else does it. God understands.

And the whole time, the pile is growing.

Ezra did not do that. He stood in the middle of his people and prayed a prayer that did not minimize anything. He named the sin. He owned it. He put his hand over his face because he could not even look up.

That is what real conviction looks like.

Not self-hatred. Not shame for shame's sake. Just an honest moment in front of a holy God where you see what you have done and you are floored that He has not already wiped you out for it.

But the prayer does not end there. A few verses down it says this.

But now we have been given a brief moment of grace, for the Lord our God has allowed a few of us to survive as a remnant.

Grace.

Even in the middle of the pile, God allowed a few to remain.

I keep wondering why those few. How were they chosen? What set them apart? Was it that they were the ones who actually recognized what was happening? The ones who blushed? The ones who could feel the weight of it?

I think there might be something to that.

Because pride does not get rescued. Pride does not even know it needs rescuing.

But the heart that bows. The heart that lifts its hand to its face and says I cannot even look at You right now, Lord. The heart that recognizes how high the pile has gotten. That heart is the kind of heart God reaches for.

Grace finds the humble.

That is the pattern through the whole Bible. The proud get sent away. The humble get pulled close.

So when I read Ezra's prayer, I am not just reading history. I am reading a posture I need to keep coming back to.

Lord, do not let me get comfortable with my sin.

Lord, let me still be able to blush.

Lord, when I see how high the pile has gotten, let me bow instead of explain.

Because the few who got to remain were probably the ones who knew they did not deserve to remain. And that is the only kind of person grace can do anything with.

When was the last time you actually felt the weight of your own sin in front of God instead of brushing it off?
