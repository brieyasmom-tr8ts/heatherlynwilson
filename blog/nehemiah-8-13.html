They Read It and Went and Did It

August 19, 2026
"On October 9 the family leaders and the priests and Levites met with Ezra to go over the Law in greater detail."Nehemiah 8:13 (NLT)

The first thing that caught my eye was the date.

October 9.

You do not always see actual dates jump out at you in the Old Testament, so when one does, it kind of stops you. October 9. A real day. People sat down in a real room and opened the Word together.

But the bigger thing that grabbed me happens right after.

They are studying. Going through the Law in greater detail. Digging in. Not just reading the surface, but actually wrestling with what it says.

And they discover something.

They were supposed to be celebrating the Festival of Shelters. It was right around the corner. And as far as anyone could remember, the people of Israel had not celebrated it the way God had instructed in a really long time.

So you know what they did?

They went and did it.

They did not put it on a list for next year. They did not form a committee to discuss the feasibility. They did not study it more and call a meeting to debate the right way to roll it out.

They just went.

They went out, gathered branches, built little shelters, lived in them for seven days, and obeyed.

I love that.

Because so often we treat reading the Bible like an end in itself. We read. We highlight. We note the things that stick out. We say that is interesting or that is convicting or I should think about that. And then we close the book and go back to our day, unchanged.

These guys did not do that.

They studied. They saw something. And they immediately moved on it.

The reading led to obedience.

I think that is one of the things we lose. We have so much access to Scripture now. We can study every word in seventeen translations. We can listen to podcasts that break it down. We can look up the original Hebrew. We can have ten different commentaries open at once.

We know more than any generation of Christians in history.

But knowing is not the point.

Doing is the point.

The people in Nehemiah 8 read the Word, saw what they were supposed to be doing, and just went and did it. That is so simple it almost feels too simple. But that simplicity might be the very thing we have lost.

When was the last time I read something in Scripture and just did it the same day? Not a week later when I felt like it. Not when it was convenient. Not after I had thought about it some more. Right then.

That is what they did.

And the result was joy. The chapter ends by saying there had not been a celebration like this since the days of Joshua. The obedience did not just check a box. It brought a fresh joy to the whole community.

That is what happens when we do what we read.

The Word stops being a study and starts being a life.

What is something you have read in the Word recently that you noticed, nodded at, and then never actually did anything about?
