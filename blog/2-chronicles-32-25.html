Highlighted

I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

2 Chronicles 32:25

July 29, 2026
"But Hezekiah did not respond appropriately to the kindness shown him, and he became proud. So the Lord's anger came against him and against Judah and Jerusalem."2 Chronicles 32:25 (NLT)

I just wrote about how much I want to be like Hezekiah and his wholeheartedness.

And then I turned the page and read this.

The same king I just held up as an example became proud. The same king who pursued God with his whole heart did not respond appropriately to the kindness God had shown him. The same king whose summary verse was about wholehearted pursuit had a moment where he forgot who got him there.

The Lord's anger came against him.

I almost did not want to write about this verse, because it complicates the picture I just painted. But honestly, I think that is part of why I highlighted it.

Because nobody is finished.

Even the people we admire have moments. Even the ones who pursue God hard for years can hit a season where pride sneaks in. Where they take a little credit they should not have taken. Where they receive blessing and forget to bow.

Hezekiah did. And he was one of the best kings in this whole story.

But here is the part I love.

The next verse.

Then Hezekiah humbled himself and repented of his pride, as did the people of Jerusalem. So the Lord's anger did not fall on them during Hezekiah's lifetime.

He humbled himself.

That is the redemption.

He did not argue with God. He did not double down. He did not justify himself. He did not blame his advisors or his circumstances or his stress. He just humbled himself and repented.

And God's anger lifted.

That is one of the most important patterns in all of Scripture. The line between the kings who finished well and the kings who finished badly is almost always this. When they were confronted with their sin, did they harden or did they humble?

Saul hardened.

Uzziah hardened.

Ahaz hardened.

Hezekiah humbled himself.

And that one difference is what made the difference.

It tells me something I needed to hear.

Pursuing God wholeheartedly does not mean I will never fall. It does not mean pride will never show up in my life. It does not mean I will not have moments where I forget who got me where I am.

What it means is that when those moments come, I do not stay there.

I humble myself.

I let God correct me. I let Him point out the pride. I let Him put me back in the right posture. I do not get defensive when He shows me something ugly. I just say you are right. I am sorry. And I keep walking.

The wholehearted pursuit is not the absence of stumbling. It is the willingness to be corrected and keep going.

That is a hopeful thing.

Because if Hezekiah, who is held up as one of the most God-seeking kings of all, had a moment of pride and still got brought back, then there is room for me when I fall too. There is room for the humble.

There just is not a lot of room for the proud who refuse to repent.

The next time God puts His finger on something in your life, will you harden or will you humble yourself?
