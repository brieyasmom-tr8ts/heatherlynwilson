The Gospel Hiding in the Law

July 15, 2026
"However, he did not kill the children of the assassins, for he obeyed the command of the Lord as written by Moses in the Book of the Law: 'Parents must not be put to death for the sins of their children, nor children for the sins of their parents. Those deserving to die must be put to death for their own crimes.'"2 Chronicles 25:4 (NLT)

The context here is a little dark. Amaziah is now king, and the men who assassinated his father have been brought to justice. He has them executed.

But he stops there.

He does not touch their children.

He could have. In that culture, it would have been completely normal to wipe out the families of your enemies. To make sure no son of an assassin grew up wanting revenge. To protect the throne by erasing the whole line.

He does not do it.

Because the law said no.

Parents must not be put to death for the sins of their children. Nor children for the sins of their parents. Those deserving to die must be put to death for their own crimes.

That law was given through Moses hundreds of years before this moment. And it was protecting the next generation in a way that the world around them was not protecting anyone.

I think this is what stood out to me when I highlighted it.

Because that principle, even buried in the law of Moses, was pointing to something much bigger.

It was pointing to Jesus.

Every person stands before God on their own. You do not get punished for what your parents did. You do not get saved by what your grandparents believed. You do not inherit someone else's righteousness. You do not inherit someone else's guilt. Each person answers for their own life.

That is the law.

But then look what God does through Jesus.

The very God who said no one can die for another person's crime is the same God who sent His Son to do exactly that.

Not because the law was broken. But because the law had a deeper truth running underneath it.

The law said no human can take another human's punishment. But Jesus was not just a human. He was the spotless Lamb of God. He was the only one who could take on the punishment of another, because He had no punishment of His own to deal with.

He did not deserve to die.

So He could die for those who did.

That is the gospel hiding inside this little verse about Amaziah.

The same God who told Israel you cannot pay for someone else's sin later said but I can. And I will.

That is what the law was always leading to.

The law showed us how impossible it was to save ourselves. It set the standard. It drew the line. It made the boundaries clear. And then Jesus came and met the standard, walked the line, and stepped across the boundary on our behalf.

I love finding little glimpses of Jesus in places I would not expect. Tucked into a chapter about a king and his enemies and a quote from an old law. The whole Bible is like that. It is one big story pointing in one direction.

His name is Jesus.

What is a place in the Old Testament where you have seen Jesus hiding in plain sight?
