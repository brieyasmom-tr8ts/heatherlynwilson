I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Marry Someone Who Loves Jesus

August 5, 2026
"For the men of Israel have married women from these people and have taken them as wives for their sons. So the holy race has become polluted by these mixed marriages. Worse yet, the leaders and officials have led the way in this outrage."Ezra 9:2 (NLT)

I was so glad to move on from Chronicles into Ezra.

And then I saw this verse I had highlighted.

Yikes.

I will be honest. This is one of those passages that can make people uncomfortable if they do not stop and look at it carefully. On the surface it sounds harsh. Like God is forbidding people from marrying outside their nationality.

That is not what is happening here. But I had to sit with it for a minute and ask myself why God was making this such a strong statement.

Here is what I landed on.

It was never about race. It was always about faith.

Israel had been called out as God's covenant people. They were set apart for Him. The whole point of their existence was to worship the one true God and to be a witness to the nations around them about who He was. And every single time Israel had married into surrounding nations, those marriages had pulled them away from God. Solomon is the most famous example. His many foreign wives turned his heart toward their gods. It happened over and over.

So when God said do not intermarry, He was not saying anything about ethnicity. He was saying do not bind yourself in the deepest commitment a human can make to someone who is going to pull you away from Me.

That is a very different sentence than how people sometimes read it.

You can see this clearly when you look at the rest of Scripture.

Ruth was a Moabite. She married Boaz, an Israelite, and was welcomed into the line of David. Because she chose Israel's God. Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God.

Rahab was a Canaanite. She joined Israel and ended up in the very lineage of Jesus. Because she chose to trust the God of Israel.

The thread is not race. The thread is faith.

That is exactly why I believe so strongly that whoever you marry should share your faith. Not just your background. Not just your culture. Not just your interests or your political views or your favorite weekend activities. Your faith.

Because marriage is the most blended life two humans can have. You are sharing a home. A bed. Money. Kids. Decisions. Time. Dreams. Sorrow. Pretty much everything. And if you are walking with God and the person beside you is not, you are going to feel that tension in every direction.

Either you will slowly drift toward them. Or you will live in a kind of split house where the deepest part of who you are is not shared with the closest person in your life.

Neither is good.

This is why I tell people you cannot just marry someone who is not bad spiritually. Or who says they believe. Or who is open to going to church sometimes. You marry someone who actually loves God. Who actually walks with Him. Who is going to pursue Him with you, even when life gets hard.

Because the seasons of life that are coming will test every shared root you have together. And the strongest shared root, by far, is a shared faith.

Ezra was not being harsh. He was protecting them.

And the same heart behind that warning is still relevant today, even if the application looks different. God still wants His people to be united with people who will help them love Him more, not less.

If you are single, this matters more than almost anything else.

If you are already married to someone who is not where you wish they were spiritually, that is a different conversation. There is hope. There is prayer. There is the witness of a wife or a husband who walks with God in front of someone who does not. The New Testament speaks directly to that.

But if you are still choosing, choose someone who loves Jesus.

The future you will thank you.

Do you believe shared faith really matters in marriage, and if so, how have you seen it play out?
