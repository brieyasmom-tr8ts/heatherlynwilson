5 Signs You're Consuming Christian Content Instead of Actually Growing

August 16, 2026

Someone once asked me how my faith had grown over the last year.

I sat there for a second because honestly, I did not know how to answer. And it made me start asking myself the question underneath the question. How would I even know?

Is it measured by how many Christian devotionals I read? The amount of the Bible I got through? The small group I showed up to? The podcasts I listened to? The sermons I did not miss?

None of those things are bad. Some of them are really good. But that question stuck with me, because I realized I had spent a whole year doing a lot of Christian things without ever really stopping to see if any of it had actually changed me.

We are in a season of the church where there is more Christian content available than at any other time in history.

More podcasts. More sermons on YouTube. More Instagram accounts posting daily devotionals. More Bible study apps. More reels of pastors preaching two minute snippets. More books. More conferences. More worship music streaming twenty four hours a day.

And on paper, that sounds great. We should be the most spiritually mature generation of Christians ever to walk the earth.

But we are not.

A lot of us are consuming more than we ever have and growing less than we ever have. And the scary part is we do not always know the difference. Consuming Christian content and actually growing in Christ can feel the same on the inside. Both feel productive. Both feel spiritual. Both feel like we are doing something with our faith.

But they are not the same.

Here are five signs you might be consuming Christian content instead of actually growing.

1. You feel spiritually full but your life is not changing.

You listen to great sermons. You save the reels. You underline the books. You highlight in your Bible. Your feed is packed with encouraging quotes and worship songs.

But if you look at your actual life over the last six months, nothing is really different. Same struggles. Same habits. Same reactions. Same relationships. Same distance from the things God has been convicting you about for years.

Consumption feels like growth because it stirs something in you. But being moved is not the same as being changed.

Real growth shows up in your life, not just in your notifications.

2. You would rather listen to someone talk about the Bible than read it yourself.

Podcasts are great. Sermons are great. Bible teachers are great. But if the only way you engage with Scripture is through someone else's mouth, you are missing the whole point.

The Bible was written for you. Not just for pastors. Not just for the people with theology degrees. God's Word is meant to be in your hands, in your language, being read by you.

If you have not opened your Bible in weeks but you have listened to hours of Christian content, that is a sign. Not to shame yourself. Just to notice. The teachers we love the most would tell us the same thing. Get in the Word for yourself.

3. You know a lot of truth but you have not obeyed the last thing God asked you to do.

You could quote verses. You could summarize sermons. You could recognize the difference between different theological views. You have knowledge.

But there is that one thing God has been asking you to do for months. The person you know you should reach out to. The habit you know you should lay down. The forgiveness you know you should extend. The obedience you know you have been putting off.

More information is not going to move you forward.

God tends to not give us new instructions until we have obeyed the last ones.

4. Your emotions get moved but your habits do not.

A powerful worship song brings tears. A sermon lights a fire in you. A verse hits you deep. You feel something. You are grateful. You mean it.

Then Monday comes and everything about how you live your life looks exactly the same. Same routines. Same distractions. Same priorities. Same lack of prayer. Same lack of time with God.

Being emotionally moved by God is a good thing. But if it never translates into different choices, different rhythms, different priorities, it is just entertainment with a Christian label.

5. You compare yourself spiritually to other people instead of measuring yourself against Jesus.

When you consume a lot of Christian content, you start to build a mental hierarchy. You know who you agree with and who you do not. You know who is theologically sharp and who is not. You know which pastors you like and which ones make you roll your eyes.

That can quietly turn into pride. You start feeling more mature because you know more. You start feeling wiser because you can spot the errors in someone else's teaching. You start measuring your walk against other Christians instead of against the standard Jesus actually set.

But the goal was never to be more informed than the person next to you.

The goal was to be more like Jesus.

So what do we do?

None of this is meant to make you feel bad. Consuming Christian content is not wrong. Learning is not wrong. Being encouraged is not wrong.

But there is a difference between input and transformation. And in a culture flooded with input, we have to be intentional about transformation.

The challenge is simple. This week, spend more time with God than you spend with content about God. Open your Bible before you open Instagram. Sit in silence with Him before you press play on the podcast. Do the last thing He asked you to do before you go looking for the next inspiring word.

Because the goal was never to consume more. The goal was to grow.

Which of these five signs is closest to where you have been living, and what is one small change you can make this week to actually grow instead of just consume?
