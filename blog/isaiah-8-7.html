Highlighted

I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Isaiah 8:7

November 6, 2026
Therefore, the Lord will overwhelm them with a mighty flood from the Euphrates River—the king of Assyria and all his glory. This flood will overflow all its channels.Isaiah 8:7 (NLT)

The context around this verse is powerful.

King Ahaz was scared of two enemy kings coming against him, and instead of trusting God, he made a deal with Assyria for protection. In the verses just before this one, God describes His own care as the gently flowing waters of Shiloah. Quiet. Peaceful. Enough. But Judah rejected it. They wanted something bigger, flashier, more powerful looking.

So God says, fine. Since you rejected the gentle waters of Shiloah, I'll let the mighty flood of the Euphrates come instead. The very nation you ran to for help will become the flood that overwhelms you.

That contrast alone is worth thinking about. God's care can look small and quiet compared to what the world offers. But the world's power always comes with a price. What we think will save us often ends up overwhelming us.

But what really caught my attention was the Euphrates itself.

I recently saw a post on social media about the Euphrates River drying up, and it prompted me to do some fact checking of my own. I didn't want to just take a post at face value and share it. What I found was pretty striking.

The Euphrates shows up all the way through the Bible. From Genesis to Revelation. Revelation 16:12 talks about the sixth angel pouring out a bowl of judgment, and the great river Euphrates dried up to prepare the way for the kings from the east. It is one of the events described in the final days before Jesus returns.

And reports over the last several years really are showing the Euphrates shrinking in ways it never has before. What was once one of the greatest rivers in the world is quietly disappearing in real time.

The same God who used the Euphrates as a symbol of overwhelming judgment in Isaiah's day is the same God who told us the Euphrates would dry up in the last days. And here we are, watching that river disappear in the news.

We don't know timelines. Only God does. And no one should be running around pointing at every world event as the exact fulfillment of prophecy. But we should be paying attention. Because the Bible has been consistent from cover to cover. The Euphrates has been part of God's story from the beginning, and it is going to be part of the ending too.

None of this is meant to scare us. Fear isn't the point. But awareness is. Because if God said the world would look a certain way before Jesus returns, and we are starting to see the world look like that, then the right response isn't panic. The right response is to make sure our hearts are ready. To be walking with Him. To be sharing the gospel. To be pouring out into the people around us while we still have time.

Because the whole Bible has been pointing to one thing all along.

Jesus is coming back.

Are you ready?
