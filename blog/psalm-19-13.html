I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Psalm 19:13

September 23, 2026
"Keep your servant from deliberate sins! Don't let them control me. Then I will be free of guilt and innocent of great sin."Psalm 19:13 (NLT)

Deliberate sin.

Not accidental or unintentional. Not the mistake you made when you were tired or unaware. Deliberate. On purpose. The kind you knew was wrong and did anyway.

David asked God to keep him from that.

And when I read it, I had to stop and actually pay attention to my own life. What are the deliberate sins I've made peace with? What are the things I know I shouldn't be doing, but I keep doing them, because I've somehow talked myself into believing they don't really count?

The little white lie to get out of something.

Gossip dressed up as just processing.

Something I took because no one would miss it.

Scrolling I wouldn't call scrolling if I really described what I was watching.

A comment about someone I'd never say to their face.

The place I let my mind go over and over that I know doesn't honor God.

Those aren't accidents.

Those are choices.

And David is asking God to keep him from being controlled by those kinds of choices. Because he knew what all of us know deep down. That deliberate sins are the ones that slowly take over, and the more often we choose them, the less they even feel like choices. Eventually the sin isn't something we do. It becomes something we are.

That is what he is asking to be delivered from.

I don't want my sins to control me.

I want the Holy Spirit to.

To be so sensitive to God's voice that the moment something starts to pull me in the wrong direction, I feel it. Not so I can beat myself up, but so I can turn quickly, catch it early, and bring it into the light before it grows roots.

Because here is what happens to us. We get calloused.

The first time you tell a small lie, it bothers you. By the tenth time, it doesn't. The first time you gossip, you feel a little pang. A hundred times later, you don't even notice. Scroll past something you know you shouldn't long enough and it just becomes what you do.

And that is where the danger is.

Not just in the sin itself. In the way we stop recognizing it as sin.

We start calling it just how I am, or not that big of a deal, or something everyone does. And all of those things get in the way of what David was asking for. That God would keep him from the things he could deliberately choose.

Because the deliberate sins are the ones that keep us from the intimacy we could have with God.

He isn't far away because of the accidents. He knows we are human and that we mess up, and He is patient and quick to forgive.

But the things we keep choosing on purpose, over and over, knowing they are wrong. Those things dull us. They quiet the voice of the Holy Spirit in us. They start to run the show if we let them.

I don't want to be run by anything but God.

So I'm praying this same prayer.

Lord, keep me from deliberate sins. Do not let them control me.

Wake me up to the things I've gone numb to. Show me the corners of my life I've made peace with when I should have surrendered. Make me sensitive again where I've gotten calloused. Let me feel the pull of Your Spirit stronger than the pull of my own habits.

And when I do stumble, let me run back to You quickly instead of letting it settle into something I stop noticing.

That is the prayer.

What deliberate sin have you gotten comfortable with lately that God might be waking you up to right now?
