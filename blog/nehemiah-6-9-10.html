When Fear Wears a Friendly Face

August 17, 2026
"They were just trying to intimidate us, imagining that they could discourage us and stop the work. So I prayed for strength to continue the work. Later I went to visit Shemaiah son of Delaiah and grandson of Mehetabel, who was confined to his home. He said, 'Let us meet together inside the Temple of God and bolt the doors shut. Your enemies are coming to kill you tonight.'"Nehemiah 6:9-10 (NLT)

Nehemiah is dealing with so much.

He is rebuilding the wall. He has enemies on every side. They have mocked him. They have plotted attacks. They have tried to lure him out into the open to ambush him. They have sent letters accusing him of rebellion to try to get him in trouble with the king.

He has faced all of it. And kept building.

But then this happens.

He goes to visit Shemaiah, someone he thought he could trust, and Shemaiah tells him your enemies are coming to kill you tonight. Let us go hide in the temple together. Bolt the doors. Stay there.

What Shemaiah was really doing was trying to manipulate Nehemiah into breaking God's law. Only priests were allowed inside the temple. If Nehemiah ran in there to save his life, he would be disobeying God, and his enemies could use that against him.

But Shemaiah said it in the most believable way. Come on, your life is in danger. Your enemies are coming. It is the safest place. Hide with me.

Imagine that.

Someone you thought was on your side, lying to your face, leaning on your fear, trying to get you to disobey God to save your own life.

That is so much harder than facing an obvious enemy.

It is one thing when the bad guys are clearly the bad guys. When the threats come from people you already know are against you, it is easier to brace yourself and keep going. But when someone you trusted starts pulling on your fear to push you somewhere you know you should not go, that is a different kind of test.

Nehemiah saw through it.

A few verses later he says should someone in my position run from danger? Should someone in my position enter the Temple to save his life? No, I will not do it.

He chose obedience to God over the safety being offered to him by a person.

I think fear is one of the most common reasons we step out of obedience.

We do not say we are scared. We say we are being wise. We say we are being practical. We say we are protecting ourselves. We say things like I just have to take care of my family. Or I cannot afford to lose this. Or if I do the right thing here, I will lose everything.

And fear pulls us into compromise.

It does not usually come from our enemies. It usually comes from someone close. Someone we trust. Someone who looks like they are looking out for us. Just hide. Just stay quiet. Just go along with it. Just protect yourself.

But obeying God is greater than fear.

Greater than the fear of losing our job. Greater than the fear of being misunderstood. Greater than the fear of being canceled, or judged, or rejected. Greater than the fear of physical harm. Greater than the fear of what might happen if we keep going when everyone around us is telling us to stop.

Nehemiah knew what he was supposed to be doing. He was supposed to be building. He was supposed to be leading. He was supposed to stay outside the temple because he was not a priest.

And he was not going to let fear change any of it.

That kind of resolve does not come naturally. He prayed for strength. He named the fear. He kept his eyes on God and not on the threats around him. He stayed put.

I want that.

I want to be a person who, when someone uses my fear to try to pull me off course, can recognize it and stay where God put me.

Even when the threat sounds real. Even when the option being offered sounds safer. Even when the voice in my ear is someone I thought I could trust.

Whose voice has been feeding on your fear lately, and is it pulling you toward God or away from Him?
