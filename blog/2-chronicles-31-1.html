Highlighted

I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

2 Chronicles 31:1

July 24, 2026
"When the festival ended, the Israelites who attended went to all the towns of Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim, and Manasseh, and they smashed all the sacred pillars, cut down the Asherah poles, and removed the pagan shrines and altars. After this, the Israelites returned to their own towns and homes."2 Chronicles 31:1 (NLT)

There is a pattern here we miss in our modern church life.

These people had just been at a huge festival. King Hezekiah had brought everyone together for Passover. It had been incredible. The Bible says there had not been a celebration like it since the days of Solomon. People worshipped. They cried out to God. They reconnected with Him after generations of drifting.

And then the festival ended.

But watch what they did next.

They did not just go home and get back to their normal life. They did not just talk about how moved they were and then forget about it by the next morning.

They left the festival and went to every town they could reach, and they tore down idols.

They smashed sacred pillars. They cut down Asherah poles. They got rid of the shrines and altars that had been quietly sitting in their towns for years. The ones nobody really talked about anymore. The ones everyone had gotten used to seeing.

And only after they had cleaned house did they go back to their own towns and homes.

That is the pattern.

Worship. Then action. Then home.

We have lost that.

We go to a great service. A conference. A revival. A retreat. A worship night that wrecks us in the best way. And then we just go home. Back to our same life. Back to our same routines. Back to the same idols we had before we walked in.

Nothing actually changes. We just felt something.

But the festival was not supposed to be the end. It was supposed to be the fuel.

The people in this story did not say that was so refreshing and then go home. They got refreshed and then they went to work. Because real worship will always lead to real change. Real encounters with God will always make you look around at the things you have been tolerating and say no more.

We have so many idols we have gotten used to.

Things we watch. Things we scroll. Things we spend on. Things we obsess over. Things we let our kids consume. Things we let into our homes that we know are not from God, but they have been there so long we barely notice them anymore. They have become the wallpaper of our lives.

These people noticed. After meeting with God, they could not unsee it.

And they tore it down.

That part really gets me.

Because what would it look like if I left worship on Sunday and actually did something? If I came home from a service that moved me and walked around my own house with new eyes and said that is going. And that is going. And we are done with this one.

Worship is supposed to change something.

If we leave church the same way we came in, week after week, year after year, that is worth paying attention to. Not because God is not moving. Because we might be receiving the encounter and skipping the response.

The order matters. Worship. Then tear down the idols. Then go home.

Not the other way around.

What is something you have gotten so used to in your life that you do not even see it as an idol anymore?
