I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Psalm 71:18

October 9, 2026
"Now that I am old and gray, do not abandon me, O God. Let me proclaim your power to this new generation, your mighty miracles to all who come after me."Psalm 71:18 (NLT)

I love this verse.

As I get older and grayer, this is exactly what I want. I want to proclaim God's power to the new generation. To tell the ones coming after me about His mighty miracles. And to make sure they know who He is and what He has done, not just in the pages of Scripture, but in my own life and the life of His followers living now.

The first half of life feels like it is about becoming. Growing up. Figuring out who you are. Starting a family. Building whatever it is you are building.

But the second half feels different.

It shifts from what am I becoming to what am I leaving behind.

Not in a sad way. In a really beautiful way. Because you start to realize that everything you have walked through, every prayer God answered, all the miracles you witnessed, every hard thing He carried you through, it wasn't just for you. It was so you would have something to hand to the ones coming next.

A story, your testimony. The record of His faithfulness.

The new generation coming up right now needs to hear it.

They are being told a lot of things by the world. They are being taught that God is optional, that faith is old fashioned, and truth is whatever you want it to be. They are being led to believe that identity comes from within, and that no one has any authority over their life but themselves.

They need to hear a different story.

They need to hear from people who have actually walked with God for decades, prayed and watched Him answer, been broken and put back together. People who have faced things that should have destroyed them and didn't, because God was there. People whose lives are living proof that He is who He says He is.

That is what David wanted to do at the end of his life.

He wasn't asking for a quiet retirement or for more comfort. He was asking to be used. Do not abandon me now. I still have something to give. Let me tell them what You have done.

I want that heart.

I want to be the kind of woman who, in her later years, is still passionately proclaiming God's power to whoever will listen. My kids. My grandkids. The younger women in my church. The strangers on the internet. Anyone God puts in front of me.

Because what I've seen God do isn't mine to keep.

It is meant to be shared, handed forward, woven into what the next generation believes about Him.

If we don't tell them, someone else will tell them something else.

That is the urgency for me. There is a whole generation growing up right now that needs to hear the stories. Not curated ones. Real ones. Ones with mess and pain and struggle in them, alongside the miracles. Because when they hit their own hard seasons, they need to know that God carries people through. Because I not just saw it, I lived it. I can point to a hundred moments in my own life where He was faithful when nothing else was.

That is what I want to leave behind.

Not just an inheritance. A testimony. A trail of stories about a God who never let me down, so they can walk into their own valleys knowing He won't let them down either.

Whose stories of God's faithfulness have shaped you, and who might be waiting to hear yours?
