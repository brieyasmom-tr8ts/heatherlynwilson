I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

2 Chronicles 33:13

August 3, 2026
"And when he prayed, the Lord listened to him and was moved by his request. So the Lord brought Manasseh back to Jerusalem and to his kingdom. Then Manasseh finally realized that the Lord alone is God!"2 Chronicles 33:13 (NLT)

This one hit me harder than most.

To give you the bigger picture, Manasseh is one of the worst kings Judah ever had. He set up idols inside the temple. He practiced sorcery. He consulted mediums. He even sacrificed his own sons in the fire. The Bible says he led Judah into doing more evil than the nations God had driven out of the land in the first place.

He was as far from God as a person could get.

And then the consequences started.

The king of Assyria came, captured him, put a hook in his nose, bound him with bronze chains, and dragged him to Babylon.

It was in that moment, sitting in chains, completely broken, that Manasseh finally prayed.

And God heard him.

That is the part of this story that I cannot get past.

The Lord listened. The Lord was moved by his request. The Lord brought him back. The very God this man had spent his whole reign rejecting was the same God who reached down into a foreign prison cell and answered him.

Then Manasseh finally realized that the Lord alone is God.

This hit a little harder for me because I have walked through something like this with one of my own. A child who chose other gods. Who followed the world for a season. Who put their hand to things they should not have put their hand to.

And then the consequences started piling up.

And in the middle of those consequences, God met them. Humbled them. Reached down into their version of Babylon and let them know He was still there. Still listening. Still able.

Their whole life changed.

I cry just thinking about it.

Because if you are a mom praying for a kid who has wandered. Or a wife praying for a husband. Or a sister praying for a brother. Or anyone who is praying for someone who looks like they are too far gone to come back, this verse is for you.

Manasseh was farther than your person.

And God heard him when he prayed.

I do not say that lightly. I know what it feels like to watch someone you love walk into things you cannot pull them out of. To pray and pray and feel like nothing is changing. To wonder if God even sees what is happening. To wonder if your person is too far now.

They are not too far.

If God could hear Manasseh in a Babylonian prison, after all the idols and the sorcery and the blood, He can hear your person too. The consequences they are walking through might be the very thing that brings them to their knees. The hook in the nose might be exactly what God uses to turn their head back toward home.

That does not make the waiting easy.

But it does make it hopeful.

Because God is not done. He is never done. He listens to prayers from prison cells. He is moved by requests from the bottom of the pit. And He brings people back from places we did not think they could come back from.

Keep praying.

Who in your life have you been praying for, and how is God inviting you to keep believing He is going to bring them back?
