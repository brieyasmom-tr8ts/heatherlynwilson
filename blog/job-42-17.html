Highlighted

I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Job 42:17

September 14, 2026
"Then he died, an old man who had lived a long, full life."Job 42:17 (NLT)

That is how the book of Job ends.

After everything. After the fire and the messengers. After the loss of every one of his children. After the sores and the ashes and the friends who couldn't help. After the chapters of pain and questions and wrestling. After hearing from God Himself out of the whirlwind. After being humbled. After being restored. After being blessed with more than he had at the beginning.

The last verse of the whole book just says this.

Then he died, an old man who had lived a long, full life.

Such a normal ending.

Almost too normal for a book that had so much drama in it. You almost expect a grand finale. Some final speech. Some soaring conclusion. Instead, it is just a quiet line about a man growing old and dying full of years.

And reading it again, I think that is why I highlighted it.

Because that is life.

Life isn't neat. It isn't one thing. It is a mix of everything. Job's life had massive tragedy in it. But it also had massive blessing. He had losses that most of us can't even fathom. But he also had restoration. He had questions that were never fully answered. And he had encounters with God that changed him forever. He had grief and joy in the same story.

And the summary isn't he suffered greatly or he was blessed greatly.

It is he lived a long, full life.

Full.

I think that is the word that stood out.

Because full doesn't mean happy every day, easy, or the absence of pain. It means whole. Complete. Rounded out with everything a real life includes. The good. The bad. The adventures. The mistakes. The happiness. The sorrow. The people you loved. The people you buried. The prayers answered. The prayers you are still waiting on.

All of it makes a full life.

I think we have a habit of trying to edit out the hard parts. We want the highlight reel, the trophies, the story that skips over the seasons of loss and pain and confusion. A life that is all Instagram and no ashes.

But that isn't a full life. That is a curated life.

Job's life was full because he didn't skip anything. He walked through the loss, the questions, the wrestling, the encounter with God, the restoration. And by the time it was all done, his life wasn't just impressive. It was full.

That is what I want.

Not a life that dodges the hard things. A life that is honest about them. A life where God is in the middle of every season, both the ones I'd choose and the ones I never would have. A life that at the end can be summed up not by the wins but by the wholeness.

She lived a long, full life.

I think that is one of the most beautiful things a person could have written about them.

It doesn't require fame. It doesn't require success as the world defines it. It doesn't require anything except the willingness to actually live the life God has for you. To let God take you through the seasons. To not run from the hard ones. To not miss the good ones. To hold it all with open hands and let Him make it into something whole.

Job could have died bitter. He had the excuse to. But he didn't. He died full.

That is the goal.

If someone summed up your life in one sentence at the end of it, would you want it to say you lived a long, full life? And what would need to change to make that true?
