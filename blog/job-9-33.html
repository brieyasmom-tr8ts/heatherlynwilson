If Only There Was a Mediator…

September 4, 2026
"If only there were a mediator between us, someone who could bring us together."Job 9:33 (NLT)

When I read this verse, it felt like Job was begging for Jesus.

He did not know His name. He did not know He was coming. He could not have pictured a manger or a cross or a resurrection. But he felt the gap so deeply that he cried out for someone, anyone, who could stand in the middle and bring God and man together.

A mediator.

Someone who could bridge the distance between a holy God and a broken human being.

Job had been pouring out his pain for chapters. Trying to make sense of his suffering. Trying to understand why God seemed so far. He kept saying things like if I could just bring my case before God. If I could just argue with Him face to face. But how can a mortal stand before the Almighty?

He felt the weight of the chasm.

And he was honest about it. He knew he was small. He knew God was holy. He knew there was no way to walk into the presence of the Almighty on his own merits and survive the encounter. The distance between them felt impossible.

So he cried out for a mediator.

If only.

Job did not know that one day, the if only would actually come.

Hundreds of years later, on a hillside in Israel, Jesus would be born. He would live. He would die. He would rise. And when He did, the very thing Job was begging for would become real. A mediator between God and man. Someone who could stand in the middle and bring us together.

The book of 1 Timothy says it plainly. There is one God and one Mediator who can reconcile God and humanity, the man Christ Jesus.

The answer to Job's prayer was Jesus.

It just took thousands of years to arrive.

That gave me chills as I read it and now as I write about it.

Because we live on the other side of that arrival. We do not have to beg for a mediator. We have one. We are not standing in Job's place wondering how a fallen person could ever come close to a holy God. We know the answer. His name is Jesus. He has already stepped into the middle. He has already done the work. He has already opened the way.

The gap that Job felt is the same gap we would feel without Christ.

I think we forget that sometimes.

We get so used to the access. We pray casually. We come to God on our own terms. We treat Him like a friend we can text any time. And He is close. He is near. He invites that kind of intimacy now.

But it cost something.

The reason we get to walk in so freely is because Jesus is standing in the middle making it possible.

Without Him, we would still be Job. Sitting in our pain and our questions, begging for someone who could bridge the gap. Knowing the holiness of God and our own brokenness in the same breath, and wondering how the two could ever come together.

But the prayer has been answered.

The mediator has come.

His name is Jesus, and He is the bridge we never could have built.

When was the last time you stopped to thank Jesus specifically for being the mediator, the One who made it possible for you to come close to God?
