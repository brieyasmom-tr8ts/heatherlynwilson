I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Psalm 50:14 & 23

October 2, 2026
"Make thankfulness your sacrifice to God, and keep the vows you made to the Most High."Psalm 50:14 & 23 (NLT)

I highlighted both of these verses in the same chapter because they are saying the same thing from two different angles.

Thankfulness as a sacrifice.

That is the phrase I keep turning over.

Because when you really think about it, thankfulness is only a sacrifice when it costs you something. When it is easy, it isn't a sacrifice. When life is going well and things are lining up, being thankful isn't really costing you anything. It is just the natural response to a good day.

But this chapter isn't talking about that kind of thankfulness.

It is talking about the kind that comes when you have to dig for it. The kind you have to choose. The kind that shows up when your circumstances would tell you to complain, or worry, or grieve, or panic, and instead you lift your eyes and say God, I still trust You. I'm still thankful.

That is a sacrifice.

Because everything in you wants to do something else. You want permission to focus on what is going wrong, to be bitter, or fearful, or angry. And in that moment, choosing thankfulness costs something. You are laying down your right to complain, your right to focus on the pain, your own understanding of the situation.

That kind of thankfulness is worship.

Not the easy kind. The hard kind. The kind that says God is still good, even when this doesn't feel good.

I think this is one of the most powerful things a believer can do. Because anybody can be thankful when things are working out. That isn't remarkable. Anybody can post a gratitude list when they just got the good news, when the vacation is booked, when the deal closed, when everyone is healthy.

But when it costs you something to give thanks, and you give thanks anyway, that is different.

That is the kind of offering God actually calls a sacrifice.

Then verse 23 takes it a step further.

Giving thanks is a sacrifice that truly honors me. If you keep to my path, I will reveal to you the salvation of God.

There is a connection here I didn't want to miss.

Thankfulness isn't just about honoring God in the moment. It is also connected to walking with Him on His path. And when we do both, He promises to reveal more of Himself to us. Specifically, His salvation. His rescue. His deliverance. The way He shows up and moves in our lives.

Which makes so much sense.

Because a heart that is constantly complaining doesn't have room to notice God. A heart that is bitter isn't looking for His hand. A heart that is fearful is too consumed with the storm to see the One walking on the water.

But a thankful heart is a heart that is watching for Him.

Thankfulness keeps our eyes open. It keeps us pointed in the right direction. It reminds us that He has done things before, so He can do them again. It positions us to notice when He is moving, because we are already looking.

And that is why staying on His path matters too. Because thankfulness without obedience is just talk. Real thankfulness leads to real trust, and real trust leads to real walking. You can't say thank You, God on Sunday and then live like you don't know Him on Monday. The two go together.

Thankfulness as a sacrifice. Obedience as a path. Salvation as the reveal.

That is the pattern God lays out here.

I want to be that kind of person.

Not fake or forced thankfulness, not the kind that shoves down the real feelings and puts on a smile. Honest thankful. The kind that can grieve and worship at the same time. The kind that can name what hurts and still name what is true about God in the same breath. The kind that keeps walking with Him even when the path is unclear, because I know He is going to reveal more of Himself as I go.

Job did it. When he lost everything, he said the Lord gave, and the Lord has taken away. Blessed be the name of the Lord. That was thankfulness as a sacrifice. It cost him. It came from a place of real pain. And it honored God more than any easy praise ever could.

Paul did it. Sitting in a prison cell, chained to a wall, he wrote about rejoicing in the Lord always. That thankfulness wasn't free. It was expensive. And it echoed through the centuries because it came from a hard place.

I want that kind of gratitude in my life. Especially in the seasons when it isn't free.

Because those are the seasons where thankfulness actually means something. And those are the seasons where God most often reveals the deeper parts of who He is.

When was the last time you gave thanks even though it cost you something? And what might God want to reveal to you on the other side of that offering?
