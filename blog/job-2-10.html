How Can a Good God…?

September 2, 2026
"But Job replied, 'You talk like a foolish woman. Should we accept only good things from the hand of God and never anything bad?' So in all this, Job said nothing wrong."Job 2:10 (NLT)

This is one of those verses we somehow miss.

Job's wife has just told him to curse God and die. She is watching her husband suffer in a way no one should have to suffer. She has lost everything too. Her grief is real, even if her advice is not great.

And Job, sitting in ashes, scraping his sores with broken pottery, having lost everything, answers her with this.

Should we accept only good things from the hand of God and never anything bad?

Plain. Direct. Not bitter. Not preachy. Just honest.

We ask versions of this question all the time. How can a good God allow bad things to happen? It is one of the most common questions in the world. Whole books have been written on it. Whole faiths have been lost over it.

And Job, in the middle of his unimaginable pain, answers it in one sentence.

He basically says, that question is foolish.

Not foolish in a mean way. He is not mocking the question. He is just saying it does not actually hold up if you stop and think about it.

Because if we are willing to take the good from God, all the blessings, the kids, the health, the provision, the joy, the answered prayers, the laughter, the love, then who are we to say He should never let anything painful touch us? If we want to call Him good when life is good, we have to be willing to acknowledge that the painful things are also passing through His hands.

He is not a vending machine that only dispenses the things we want.

He is God.

That sounds harsh, but it is actually one of the most freeing truths in the Bible.

Because if God can be trusted with the good and not the hard, then He is not really God. He is just a wish fulfillment system. And if He is just a wish fulfillment system, then the moment something hard happens, the whole faith falls apart.

But if He is actually God, the kind who is sovereign over all of life, then He can hold both the good and the painful with wisdom we do not have.

Job did not understand what was happening to him. He had no idea about the conversation between God and Satan that we get to read in chapters 1 and 2. He had no behind the scenes view. He just had loss. Devastating loss.

And he still said the same God who gave is the same God who takes.

That is faith.

That does not mean we have to pretend the hard things are not hard. Job grieved. He wept. He sat in ashes for a week with his friends in silence. He later asked some really raw questions in the chapters that follow. He was not a robot. He was a man in deep pain.

But underneath all of it, he held on to something I think we are losing in our generation.

God is God in the good and in the bad.

Not the cause of every bad thing. Not the author of evil. But sovereign over the world He created, in ways we do not always understand.

And if we are going to trust Him at all, we have to trust Him in both.

I do not want to be the kind of believer who only thanks God when life is going well. I want to be the kind who can stand in the middle of loss and still say He gave. He has taken. Blessed be His name.

The same God. In both seasons.

When was the last time something hard happened in your life and your first reaction was to question God instead of trust Him?
