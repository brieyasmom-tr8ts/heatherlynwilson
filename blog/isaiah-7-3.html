Highlighted

I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Isaiah 7:3

November 4, 2026
Then the Lord said to Isaiah, "Take your son Shear-jashub and go out to meet King Ahaz. You will find him at the end of the aqueduct that feeds water into the upper pool, near the road leading to the field where cloth is bleached."Isaiah 7:3 (NLT)

I didn't know Isaiah had a son.

That was the first thing that caught me. I've read Isaiah more than a few times, but I never really stopped to think about the fact that he was a dad. The book is so full of visions and prophecies and warnings and Messianic promises that it is easy to forget there was a real man behind those words. A husband. A father. Someone with kids running around at home.

He wasn't just a prophet. He was a person.

And then I got to his son's name, and that made it even better.

Shear-jashub. It means a remnant will return.

That isn't a coincidence. In the middle of a season where God was warning of judgment and enemies coming against Judah, He had Isaiah name his son something that carried a promise. A remnant will return. Even when things fall apart, God will preserve His people. Even when nations rise and fall, He is going to keep a thread of His people alive.

Every time Isaiah called his son's name, he was speaking hope out loud. Every time he yelled dinner or come here or time for bed, he was reminding himself and everyone within earshot of what God had promised.

That is a powerful use of a name.

Names in the Bible were never random. They carried meaning. They marked something. They pointed to something bigger than the person carrying them.

I love that God gave Isaiah, a man delivering hard messages, a son whose name was full of hope. It was almost like a personal reminder for the prophet himself. When the days felt heavy and the warnings he had to give felt hopeless, his own son was walking around as living proof that God wasn't done. A remnant will return.

I think about the names we give our kids and the meaning we may or may not put behind them. Some pick names because they sound pretty or because they're family names. Others pray over the meaning, feeling like God is speaking something over the child before they're even born.

There is nothing wrong with any of those. But I love that Isaiah's family carried a story. His son wasn't just his son. He was a message.

If your name had to speak something over your life, what would you want it to say?
