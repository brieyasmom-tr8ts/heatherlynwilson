I read the entire Bible in January. Now I'm revisiting my highlights and blogging about why each verse stood out.

Should I Bow Down to a Piece of Wood?

November 23, 2026
"Such stupidity and ignorance! Their eyes are closed, and they cannot see. Their minds are shut, and they cannot think. The person who made the idol never stops to reflect, 'Why, it's just a block of wood! I burned half of it for heat and used it to bake my bread and roast my meat. How can the rest of it be a god? Should I bow down to worship a piece of wood?'"Isaiah 44:18-19 (NLT)

This passage is honestly a little funny when you actually picture it.

God is basically saying, do you not realize you made the idol yourself? You took a block of wood, burned half of it to cook your dinner, and warmed yourself by the fire it made. And then you took the other half of the same block of wood and carved it into a god you now bow down to and worship. Do you not see how absurd this is?

When you spell it out that plainly, it sounds ridiculous. Because it is.

But the deeper thing this passage is getting at is that people without the Lord can't see it. Their eyes are closed and their minds are shut. They literally can't think through the absurdity of what they are doing. The wisdom to see it clearly isn't there because the fear of the Lord isn't there.

This is what happens when God isn't central in a person's life. They start to worship things they made themselves. Not always wooden idols. It might be a career they built, a lifestyle they curated, or a version of themselves they carved out with their own hands and then started bowing down to.

The Bible isn't just describing ancient people with wooden statues. It is describing the human heart. The same pattern happens over and over in every generation. We take things that came from us or through us and we make them the center of our lives. Then we can't even see how backwards it is because our minds are shut to the truth.

That should make us all take stock.

What have I made with my own hands that I have quietly started to worship, and what am I bowing down to that God gave me the ability to create in the first place?

Where has my thinking gotten shut down because I am not walking with the Lord in the area?

The absurd part of this story is what makes it uncomfortable. Because we would never bow down to a piece of wood. But there are things we bow down to that, if we could see them clearly, would look just as ridiculous.

The good news is that the very next verses in this chapter shift. God says, remember these things, and don't forget who you belong to. He offers to open eyes that have been shut and to bring back people who have wandered.

His invitation to see clearly is always open.

Is there something in your life you have quietly been worshiping that if you saw it clearly, would look just as absurd as bowing down to a block of wood?
