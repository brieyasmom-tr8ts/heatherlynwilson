Highlighted: What Rises in Pride

July 17, 2026
"But when he had become powerful, he also became proud, which led to his downfall. He sinned against the Lord his God by entering the sanctuary of the Lord's Temple and personally burning incense on the incense altar."2 Chronicles 26:16 (NLT)

What we see playing out in our culture today is not new.

That is the first thing I thought reading this.

This is King Uzziah. He started off as a good king. He sought God. He listened to wise counsel. God's hand was on him in a powerful way. He won battles. He built things. He grew strong. The Bible literally says his fame spread far and wide because God helped him in marvelous ways.

But then.

When he had become powerful, he also became proud, which led to his downfall.

It is the same story we see over and over.

God puts His hand on someone's ministry. They start gaining a following. People listen to them. People love them. The platform grows. The influence grows. And somewhere along the way, they forget where the power came from in the first place.

They start to think the rules do not apply to them anymore.

That is what happened with Uzziah.

He walked into the temple to burn incense himself. Which sounds harmless. Right? He was the king. He loved God. He was worshipping. Why would burning incense be a bad thing?

Because only the priests were allowed to do it.

God had been very clear about that. There was a specific role for the king and a specific role for the priest. Uzziah knew it. Everyone knew it.

But he had gotten powerful enough that he thought he could redraw the line.

The priests confronted him. Eighty of them. Brave men of God who walked right up to the king and said this is not for you to do. Leave the sanctuary.

And here is the part that breaks me.

He did not humble himself.

He got angry. He puffed up. He stood there in the middle of the temple, holding the incense, and basically said I am the king. I can do this if I want to.

And in that exact moment, leprosy broke out on his forehead.

His life was destroyed in front of everyone.

He was rushed out of the temple. He lived in isolation the rest of his life. His son had to take over the kingdom for him. The reign that had been so blessed ended in shame.

I want to be careful with this story, because it is easy to read it and just point fingers at famous pastors and influencers who have fallen. And there have been a lot. The pattern is the same. God's hand. A following. Pride. A line crossed. A public collapse.

We do not have leprosy now. We have social media. We have headlines. We have receipts and screenshots and stories that go everywhere within hours.

The exposure looks different. But the principle is the same.

What rises in pride will fall in public.

And the warning is not just for the famous person. It is for me too.

Because I am not above this pattern. None of us are.

The moment I start thinking I have earned what God gave me, I am one step from where Uzziah was. The moment I start thinking the rules apply to everyone except me, I am right there. The moment I get corrected and respond with anger instead of humility, I am holding the incense.

God resists the proud.

Not in a small way. Not in a quiet way. He actively resists.

I want to be the kind of person who, when corrected, drops the incense and walks out.

Not the kind who argues my way into a downfall.

When was the last time you were corrected, and how did you actually respond?
