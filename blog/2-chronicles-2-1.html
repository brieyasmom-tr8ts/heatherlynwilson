Solomon Decided

July 1, 2026
"Solomon decided to build a Temple to honor the name of the Lord, and also a royal palace for himself."2 Chronicles 2:1 (NLT)

If you read my last blog, you will get why this verse caught me.

David did basically everything but the building itself. He had the plans. He organized the materials. He set aside the gold and silver. He passed on the instructions to Solomon and said the Lord gave me every detail of this in writing.

David did the work behind the work.

Then I turn the page into 2 Chronicles, just a few chapters later, and the first verse says this.

Solomon decided to build a Temple to honor the name of the Lord, and also a royal palace for himself.

Solomon decided.

That phrasing is what got me.

It is almost like whoever wrote 2 Chronicles is picking up the story without the full backstory. Like they are just recording what they knew. Solomon decided to start building. That was the moment from their angle.

But we, the readers, just got done with 1 Chronicles. We know it was not really Solomon's decision in the way it sounds. We know David had been preparing for this for years. We know God told David the plans Himself. We know Solomon was inheriting all of it.

And yet right here, the verse just credits Solomon with the decision.

It is a good reminder that the Bible is real history written by real people. Not every chapter has the full picture. Some chapters are someone telling what they saw. Some chapters are someone giving the highlights. Some chapters are someone filling in details that were missing.

That is part of what makes Scripture so rich. You have to read it together, not in isolated pieces. One book gives you part of the story. Another book gives you the rest.

If you only had 2 Chronicles, you might think Solomon woke up one day and decided to build a temple all on his own.

But because we have 1 Chronicles, we know better. We know the years of preparation. We know the father who prayed and planned and passed it on. We know it was God's idea long before it was anyone's decision.

It also makes me think about how God works in ways that span generations. The temple was on God's heart long before Solomon was born. David carried it. Solomon built it. And somewhere in heaven, God knew the full thread from beginning to end.

That is the bigger story. Not who got the credit. Not who got the headline. Just God moving across generations to do what He set out to do.

And He is still doing that.

He is still working in ways that started before us and will continue after us. He is still weaving plans through families and prayers and quiet faithfulness. He is still the one whose idea it all was in the first place.

Have you ever realized that something good in your life was actually started by someone before you, who never got to see how it played out?
